    The calorie tracker that reads your plate

    Take one photo. Kalorat names every part of the meal, weighs it in grams and adds it to your day. Free, open source, no subscription.

    Download for Android Install on iPhone

    Point at a part of the plate, or pick another meal.

    Every part, weighed on its own

    Kalorat does not guess one number for the plate. It finds each part, estimates its grams and looks up what a gram of it is worth. Then it adds them up.

      Together 658 kcal

      Three estimates, the middle one counts

      The photo goes to Gemini three times at once, so it takes about as long as once. Kalorat keeps the estimate in the middle: one odd guess can’t move your day.

      Your day, as a climb

      Each meal is a step up the trail. The dashed line is your goal; the number says how much is left. Kalorat sets the goal from your height, weight, age, activity and whether you want to lose, keep or gain.

      What happens after the shutter

      1. 1

        Point it at the plate

        The frame finds the meal or a barcode. Several photos of one meal are fine: the side, the label, the half you already ate.

      2. 2

        Say what the camera can’t see

        “Tofu fried in a tablespoon of oil” goes along with the photo. A note is optional; it covers what the camera can’t see, like the oil in the pan.

      3. 3

        Three estimates at once

        The parts appear as Gemini names them, and the first of three estimates lands within seconds. All three run side by side, so three take no longer than one.

      4. 4

        Every part, in grams

        You see how sure it is, the range it could be, and why. Too much? One tap for lower, one for higher, or change a single part.

      No plate to photograph?

      • Photograph it

        A plate, a tray, a bowl. Several photos, one meal.

      • Scan the barcode

        Packaged food comes with its label values from Open Food Facts. Not found? Photograph the label.

      • Type it

        Write what you ate in your own words: “2 eggs, a slice of toast with butter”.
